Draw a deterministic pushdown automaton and lock it in, then give it inputs and watch it run with its stack. Each rule a, x → y reads a, pops x and pushes y, and any of the three can be ε. The stack starts empty. At most one move may ever apply, and when none does, the DPDA rejects.

One string per line; ε is the empty string.

A DPDA reads its input left to right and makes at most one move at a time: no two of a state's rules may ever apply together. Two rules clash when their read symbols match (or one reads ε) and their pop symbols match (or one pops ε). When no move applies, the DPDA rejects. It accepts if it reads all of the input and is then in an accept state, whatever is left on the stack.
